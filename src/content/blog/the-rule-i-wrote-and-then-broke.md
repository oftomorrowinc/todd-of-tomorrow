*From the vault. Written now, about 2023.*

In January 2023 I wrote down eight rules for how I wanted to work that
year. I still think seven of them are right. Here is number five:

> A project isn't done until it is shared with the world.

Between writing that sentence and today I started a hundred and eighteen
new repositories. I shipped almost none of them to anybody. A publishing
system for independent authors. A markup language for interactive audio. A
fact-checking pipeline that went through a whole non-fiction book claim by
claim. A translation system. A protocol that lets an app run on your
computer instead of on my bill. You have not heard of any of it, because I
did not tell you.

Here is the part that makes it funny. I used to be good at this. Between
2008 and 2017 I published two hundred and ten posts – a blog, then a
Tumblr. The first post on that blog is called "Blogging Again," and it
opens with me hoping the third time's the charm and thanking a friend for
pushing me to start. The only comment on it, three days later, is that
friend saying he was happy to kick me back into it, because I had too many
good ideas not to share. I wrote sixty-five posts that year. In 2008 I
wrote one arguing that publishing what you learn is how you keep from
losing it, and how you get other people to improve it. I believed that. I
was right.

Then I stopped, in 2017, and did not publish anything for nine years. Nine
years on, nine years off. And near the end of that quiet I sat down and
wrote myself a rule I had already known and already followed, as though
the thought were new. So the pattern, as far back as the record goes, is:
someone pushes me, I turn out to be prolific, I stop, and a few years
later I decide it is time to start sharing my work again. This is at least
the fourth time.

I want to be careful about how I tell this, because there are two easy
versions and both are wrong. The flattering one is that I was heads-down,
quietly compounding, and now I emerge with the goods. The self-flagellating
one is that I lost my nerve. What actually happened is simpler. I wanted
this for years. I wrote it down twice. I was also exactly where I wanted
to be – at home, while my kids grew up – and I wasn't leaving early. Both
were true, and one of them had an end date. I said last week what that
date was.

The evidence is unusually complete, because I keep everything. I can tell
you the month I first noticed the trade had a cost – November 2022 –
because I wrote it down at the time:

> By not focusing on business, I have had more time at home with the kids.
> However, it has kept me from meeting new people and making new friends…
> Time to fix this now.

"Now" took four years. I am not going to dress that up, and I am not going
to apologise for it either. It was the deal, and I would take it again.

But here is the one I cannot explain away. In October 2023 – ten months
after the rule – I sat down to start a YouTube channel, and I wrote the
introduction. It opens: *"So why is this guy starting a YouTube channel?
Honestly, I have wanted to for years. I build tons of cool stuff… but then
never share anything beyond that."* It runs to nineteen thousand words.
Revision 1,306. The whole story, everything I had built and why, written
out for an audience of nobody, because I then did not start the channel.
In August of this year there is a note in my files titled "Todd Of
Tomorrow Channel." Still planning it.

So this is the first of a run of posts out of the vault. Real dates, real
work, including the parts that did not work – and there is a lot of that.
I was, and am, scared to put myself out there like this. I have to get the
fuck over it. Publishing the rule I broke seemed like the only honest way
to start doing what the rule says.
